Before you go

A word about Cape Ann

Gloucester and Rockport sit on the same peninsula, about fifteen minutes apart, so it is tempting to try to do everything in one go. Resist that.

Pick a theme, not a checklist

Cape Ann is best explored through themed itineraries. Choose whale watching and Gloucester Harbor, historic houses and castles, or Rockport and Halibut Point. Trying to include every attraction in one day will feel rushed.

Each of the itineraries below is built to stand on its own as a full, unhurried day. If you have two days on Cape Ann, do two of them properly rather than all three badly.

Itinerary one

Gloucester: whales and the harbor

America's oldest seaport, a working fishing fleet, and humpbacks feeding on Stellwagen Bank a few miles offshore.

Drive from Winthrop
Approximately 45–90 minutes, depending on traffic
Best season
Roughly mid-April to mid/late October
Good for
Families, wildlife, seafood

What to see

  • 7 Seas Whale Watch — departs from Seven Seas Wharf on Rogers Street. Cruises run about 3–4 hours.
  • Gloucester HarborWalk — a free, self-guided waterfront route marked by 42 granite story posts, each with a QR code that unlocks audio and video. Starts at St. Peter's Square.
  • The Fishermen's Memorial — the "Man at the Wheel" on Stacy Boulevard, unveiled in 1925 for the town's 300th anniversary. The plaques nearby name more than 5,000 Gloucester fishermen lost at sea since 1716.
  • Eastern Point and the Dog Bar Breakwater — a 2,250-foot granite jetty with open-ocean views. Please read the note below before you go.

Booking the cruise. Departure times vary by date rather than following a fixed daily timetable, so book ahead and confirm your departure time on your reservation. Bring layers and a warm jacket even in July, plus sunscreen and motion-sickness tablets if you're prone to it. Card machines don't work offshore — carry cash for the snack bar. Schedules vary by season; confirm current hours and reservation requirements on the official website.

Eastern Point Lighthouse — please read

Eastern Point Light is one of Gloucester's iconic coastal sights, but it is not a place you can freely wander.

  • The lighthouse tower, keeper's dwelling and the fenced station grounds are closed to the public. The light is an active aid to navigation operated by the U.S. Coast Guard. Please do not enter the fenced area.
  • The Dog Bar Breakwater and the shoreline path are open, and walking the breakwater is the real reason to come.
  • Eastern Point Boulevard is a private road through a residential neighborhood. Visitors are permitted through to the lighthouse parking area and the Mass Audubon sanctuary, but you are a guest — drive slowly, don't park in front of homes, and stay off private lawns.
  • Parking is very limited. The small lot is run by Mass Audubon for sanctuary visitors; members park free and non-members pay a daily vehicle fee. It fills quickly in summer, and parking at the gate is not permitted.
  • The breakwater granite is uneven, gapped and slippery, with no railings. Wear sturdy closed shoes and skip it in high wind, storm surf, or icy conditions.

Access rules can change. Please check the current guidance before you set out.

Itinerary two

Beauport and Hammond Castle

A second, entirely different Gloucester day — interiors, invention, and two houses built by men with more imagination than restraint.

Drive from Winthrop
Approximately 45–90 minutes, depending on traffic
Best season
Late spring through autumn
Good for
Architecture, design, history, rainy days

Beauport, the Sleeper-McCann House

The summer home of Henry Davis Sleeper, one of America's first professional interior designers, begun in 1908 and expanded into a warren of 56 rooms — each staged to evoke a different historical or literary theme. It is effectively a lifetime's collecting turned into architecture. A National Historic Landmark, now cared for by Historic New England.

  • Interior design as the whole point — this is a house about rooms, not furniture
  • Colorful and highly individual spaces, one opening into the next
  • Decorative arts, glass, ceramics, folk art and architectural salvage
  • Ocean views from a rock ledge above the harbor, and an Arts and Crafts landscape of garden "rooms"

Guided tours only — there is no self-guided wandering, and group sizes are limited. The standard house tour runs about an hour. Longer formats are offered, including a behind-the-scenes tour of passageways and closets normally closed to visitors.

Hammond Castle Museum

A medieval-style castle on the Gloucester shoreline, built roughly a century ago by the inventor John Hays Hammond Jr. The museum describes itself as STEAM-inspired, and the mix is genuinely odd in the best way: antiquities alongside early twentieth-century technology and Hammond's own inventions.

  • Medieval-inspired architecture with stone interiors and a courtyard
  • An important pipe organ
  • Historic collections and architectural stonework throughout
  • Ocean views, and a genuinely dramatic castle-like visit

Two rules that catch people out at Hammond Castle. Visitors arriving by personal vehicle must buy admission in advance online — those arriving on foot, by bicycle, rideshare or public transport may buy walk-in admission. And street parking is no longer permitted on Hesperus Avenue; the museum lot is small, so arrive early.

Itinerary three

Rockport and Halibut Point

A working art colony at the end of Cape Ann, and a granite quarry on the edge of the Atlantic.

Drive from Winthrop
Approximately 50–75 minutes, longer summer weekends
Best season
Late spring through October
Good for
Art, walking, photography, seafood

Halibut Point State Park

The Babson Farm Quarry sits about a half mile from the parking lot, a deep pool of green water rimmed with cut granite and open ocean beyond. Rockport granite paved city streets and built Boston's Custom House Tower. A 60-foot WWII fire-control tower now forms part of the visitor center, and you can climb it for the panorama — on a clear day you can see up the coast into Maine.

Walking at Halibut Point

  • Trail difficulty: easy to moderate — a roughly 1.7-mile loop on wide, mostly level paths. This is not a mountain hike.
  • Suggested time: 1.5–2.5 hours with the visitor center and tide pools
  • Highlights: the granite quarry, rocky coastline, ocean views, tide pools
  • Footwear: sneakers or walking shoes — the rock underfoot is uneven
  • Children: appropriate with close supervision; the tide pools are a genuine draw
  • Important: use caution near quarry edges and on the rocky shoreline. Rock below the high-tide line is genuinely slippery. Swimming and entering the quarry are prohibited, and there are no lifeguards.

Parking is charged seasonally, at a lower rate for Massachusetts residents, and is free for pedestrians and cyclists year round. In peak summer the lot closes temporarily when it fills — arrive early.

Rockport is an art colony, not just a shopping street

It is easy to mistake Rockport for a pretty row of gift shops. It isn't. The town's official tourism body counts more than 30 galleries representing 400-plus artists, and calls it one of the oldest art colonies in America.

  • Painters — the Cape Ann tradition runs through Aldro Hibbard, Anthony Thieme and W. Lester Stevens; Winslow Homer and Edward Hopper both worked here
  • Photographers and craftspeople — the Art Association maintains a distinct photography membership
  • Working galleries and studios, many along Bearskin Neck
  • Motif No. 1 — the red shack on Bradley Wharf, often called the most-painted building in America. Worth knowing: the 1884 original was destroyed in the Blizzard of 1978, and what you photograph today is an exact replica rebuilt that same year.

The Rockport Art Association & Museum on Main Street has been at the center of this since 1921 and runs 40-plus exhibitions a year across three buildings. Admission is free. It is closed Mondays.

An old fact worth retiring. Rockport was famously a dry town from 1856 — the result of Hannah Jumper and some two hundred women taking hatchets to the town's liquor supply. That ended long ago: restaurants have served alcohol since the mid-2000s. The one place where "bring your own" still applies is The Lobster Pool.

Itinerary four

Salem

Two very different towns share one address: a serious maritime and art city for eleven months, and the busiest Halloween destination in the country for the twelfth.

Drive from Winthrop
Roughly 30–45 minutes; far longer October weekends
By train
About 32 minutes, North Station to Salem
Good for
History, art, families, rainy days

Salem outside October

Come in May or June and you get the same museums with a fraction of the crowd.

  • Peabody Essex Museum — the big one, and the best rainy-day option in Salem by a distance. Maritime art, Asian export art, fashion and design, plus Yin Yu Tang, a 200-year-old Chinese house reassembled on site. Closed Tuesdays and Wednesdays — the single most common way to wreck a Salem day trip.
  • House of the Seven Gables — the 1668 mansion that inspired Hawthorne's novel, plus his birthplace moved onto the same campus. Reservations required, and tickets release monthly. Best for history, architecture and literature.
  • Salem Witch Museum — a seated presentation running every half hour, timed entry. Good for families and for witch-trial history; arrive early, as late arrivals aren't reseated.
  • The Witch House — the home of trial judge Jonathan Corwin, and one of the very few structures still standing in Salem with direct ties to 1692. Accused people were brought here for pretrial examination. Small, quick, and architecturally the real thing.
  • Salem Maritime National Historical Park — nine acres and twelve historic structures including the 1819 Custom House where Hawthorne worked. Free. Derby Wharf and the grounds are open around the clock.
  • Essex Street pedestrian mall and the historic waterfront — free, open, and the connective tissue between everything above.
  • Walking tours — twenty-plus operators covering witch-trial history, general history, ghosts, food and family routes.

Check the day of the week before you drive. The Peabody Essex Museum is closed Tuesdays and Wednesdays; the Rockport Art Association is closed Mondays; several Salem restaurants close early in the week. A Tuesday trip to Salem is still worthwhile — but plan it around the Witch House, Salem Maritime and the waterfront rather than PEM.

October

Visiting Salem During Halloween

Salem's month-long Haunted Happenings draws over a million visitors. It is genuinely wonderful and genuinely overwhelming.

Through October the whole city turns over to it: Haunted Happenings, costumes everywhere, street performers, seasonal markets, decorations, special walking tours, evening events and the marketplace at Derby Square.

Be realistic about the crowds. October weekends — and especially the last two weekends and Halloween itself — are extremely busy. Salem's October visitor numbers have been breaking records year on year. If you want atmosphere without gridlock, a weekday in early October is a completely different experience from the Saturday before Halloween.

How to do October well

  • Visit on a weekday if you possibly can.
  • Arrive early in the morning.
  • Reserve museums and tours well in advance — several sell out, and the Witch Museum sells October tickets online only.
  • Take the commuter rail on the busiest October weekends. The MBTA adds service on the Newburyport/Rockport line every weekend in October.
  • Expect waits at restaurants, and eat outside peak hours.
  • Wear comfortable walking shoes — Salem is cobbles and brick.
  • Check this year's Haunted Happenings schedule before you plan a specific day.
  • Do not count on finding convenient downtown parking.

Parking and getting in during October

The city runs free shuttles on October weekends between satellite parking lots and downtown, with well over a thousand free spaces at the outer lots. Downtown-adjacent streets switch to resident permit parking, and rates at city garages, lots and meters change on October weekends and holidays. Road closures come and go around events. Details are published closer to the season — check the city's October parking and transportation page before you travel.

Dates change every year, so we deliberately don't list them here. Check the official schedule for the current season.

Getting there without a car. Salem has the best public-transport case of any trip on this page. Itinerary five

Crane Beach and Castle Hill

Four miles of barrier beach and dunes in Ipswich, with a Stuart-style mansion on the hill above it.

Drive from Winthrop
About 45–60 minutes; 75–90+ on summer weekends
Best season
Late August into September
Good for
Beach days, dunes, grand houses

One thing to get straight first: Crane Beach is in Ipswich, not Gloucester. It is often lumped in with Cape Ann, but it's a separate trip up Route 133, and the approach down Argilla Road is its own bottleneck.

Book your parking before you go

This is the single most important thing on this page. Advance timed-entry passes are required on weekends and strongly encouraged every other day. In summer, passes are released Mondays at noon for the coming Tuesday to Thursday, and Thursdays at noon for Friday to Monday. Without a pass you can simply be turned away once the lot is full — and it fills early. Advance parking reservations, seasonal access rules, admission requirements and beach conditions all change; check the official Crane Beach and Trustees websites before traveling.

Three things that surprise first-time visitors

  • Greenhead flies. They breed in the salt marsh and bite hard, roughly from mid-July into the first week of August, worst mid-morning and late afternoon. The Trustees run a beach information line with same-day conditions — call it on the morning of a July visit. This is exactly why late August and early September are the sweet spot: warmest water of the year, no greenheads.
  • Piping plovers. Crane is one of the world's most important nesting sites for them. Fenced sections of the upper beach are closed through nesting season, roughly April to September, and the closures shift week to week. Stay out of fenced areas and off the wrack line where the chicks feed.
  • No dogs from April through September. None, at all. Dogs are only permitted October through March.

Castle Hill on the Crane Estate

Above the beach sits a 165-acre estate built by Richard and Florence Crane. The Great House is a Stuart-style mansion by architect David Adler, finished in 1928 and a National Historic Landmark. The showpiece is the Grand Allée, a half-mile sweep of lawn running from the house straight down to the sea, with restored Italian and Rose gardens either side.

The grounds, gardens and trails are open daily, year round. The Great House opens seasonally from mid-April through October plus select dates around the holidays. Grounds admission is first-come, first-served; guided tours are ticketed separately and advance tickets are recommended. Crane Beach and Castle Hill are part of the same estate but are ticketed separately.

Bikes travel free on the train and the ride from the station is pleasant.

Shorter outings

More day trips

Smaller ideas for when you don't want to commit to a whole Cape Ann day.

Newburyport & Plum Island

Why go: a compact red-brick downtown around Market Square opening onto a riverside boardwalk; the Custom House Maritime Museum in an 1835 building; and the barrier-island beaches, salt marsh and nationally significant birding of the Parker River National Wildlife Refuge.

Best season: late spring, and especially September–October. See the warning below — midsummer is the worst time for the refuge even though it's the best beach weather.

Travel: roughly 45–60 minutes by car, up to an hour and a half on summer weekends. The commuter rail reaches Newburyport, but downtown is about a mile from the station (the Clipper City Rail Trail connects them). Plum Island is not practically reachable by transit — drive or bike.

Eat: Sea Level Oyster Bar on Market Square, or Michael's Harborside on the water.

Parker River refuge Directions

Concord & Lexington

Why go: the Old North Bridge and the five-mile Battle Road Trail at Minute Man National Historical Park; literary Concord — Alcott's Orchard House, the Concord Museum, and Authors Ridge where Alcott, Emerson, Thoreau and Hawthorne lie within yards of each other; and Walden Pond, with a swimming beach and shoreline loop.

Best season: late spring and September–October. Ranger programs run daily from Memorial Day weekend through October.

Travel: about 40–55 minutes by car, longer in rush hour. The Fitchburg line reaches Concord and the town center is walkable from the depot — but the Battle Road, the visitor center and Walden Pond are not. Lexington has no commuter rail.

Walden Pond parking: no advance reservation is needed for individuals — but the lot closes once the reservation hits capacity, which happens routinely on warm days, and a pre-paid pass won't get you in. Arrive early and check park alerts.

Eat: Main Streets Market & Cafe for something casual, or Concord's Colonial Inn on Monument Square.

Minute Man NHP Directions

Portsmouth, New Hampshire

Why go: Strawbery Banke, a ten-acre outdoor living-history neighborhood where the historic houses stand on their original sites; a dense, walkable brick downtown around Market Square; and Prescott Park on the Piscataqua, free, with display gardens and a summer arts festival.

Best season: late June to early October — September into early October is the sweet spot, with the museum and harbor cruises still running and thinner crowds.

Travel: the longest trip here, roughly an hour and five to an hour and twenty by car, and up to two hours on summer weekends. No commuter rail and no Amtrak stop in Portsmouth — the practical car-free option is the C&J bus from Boston or Logan.

Eat: Row 34 for oysters at lunch or dinner, or Jumpin' Jay's Fish Café for dinner (dinner only, reservations advised).

Marblehead

Why go: the closest trip on this page. Abbot Hall displays Archibald Willard's original The Spirit of '76 — free to see. Old Town has around 300 surviving pre-Revolutionary houses on winding streets, with harbor panoramas from Crocker and Fountain Parks. Fort Sewall, on a fortified site dating to 1644, is a free clifftop park at the end of Front Street.

Best season: Memorial Day to Labor Day for full access; September and early October for the same thing with easier parking. May is peak spring bird migration on Marblehead Neck.

Travel: about 30–45 minutes by car. There is no commuter rail to Marblehead — the branch line closed in 1959. Buses run to Wonderland for the Blue Line, but driving is far simpler.

Parking is genuinely difficult in Old Town, and the Devereux Beach lot is resident-only. Park outside the centre and walk — the town is small and rewards wandering.

Eat: The Landing (harbor views, takes reservations) or The Barnacle (on the water since 1961, walk-in).

Abbot Hall Directions

Manchester-by-the-Sea

Why go: Singing Beach, named for sand that genuinely squeaks underfoot when dry. It is one of very few New England ocean beaches you can reach properly by train — the station is a flat half-mile walk away, passing Masconomo Park. Coolidge Reservation nearby has a free oceanfront Great Lawn.

Best season: late June to early September for swimming; September and October for quiet.

Travel: roughly 40–55 minutes by car, longer on summer weekends. Take the train instead — nonresident parking is weekdays only in season, and weekends are resident-sticker only. A walk-on beach fee applies to most visitors in staffed season; bring cash.

Eat: 35 Beach for dinner (closed Mon–Tue), or Captain Dusty's Ice Cream on the walk back to the station.

Directions

Boston Harbor Islands

Why go: Fort Warren on Georges Island, with its parade ground and dark tunnel (bring a flashlight), and Spectacle Island's accessible perimeter trail and swimming beach. Peddocks is reachable from Hingham.

Best season: daily ferries mid-June to early September; September into mid-October is quieter but runs fewer days.

Travel: ferries leave from Long Wharf North in Boston, roughly 15–25 minutes' drive from Winthrop — but downtown parking is dire, so take the Blue Line to Aquarium instead. Seasonal service only, so check the schedule first. Peddocks has limited capacity; reserve early.

Eat: pack your own. There is drinking water on Spectacle Island, but no food concession is confirmed for this season on either island.

Ferry schedule

Closer than any of these: Deer Island is physically connected to Winthrop — no ferry, no fare, no parking problem. Open sunrise to sunset, free, with a 2.6-mile paved accessible loop and the best skyline view on this list. If a full day trip feels like too much, walk that instead.

Finally

A few planning notes

  • Check opening days before you drive. The Peabody Essex is closed Tuesday and Wednesday; the Rockport Art Association is closed Monday; Feather & Wedge is closed Monday and Tuesday. A wasted hour on Route 128 is a sad way to start a day.
  • Book the things that can sell out. Whale watches, Crane Beach parking, Beauport tours, Hammond Castle if you're driving, and anything in Salem in October.
  • Leave early on summer weekends. Route 128 north on a Saturday morning, and the return south on Sunday afternoon, are both slower than the map suggests.
  • Seasonal means seasonal. Several of the best places on this page — the whale watch, Roy Moore, The Lobster Pool, the island ferries, the Rockport and Ipswich shuttles — close for the winter or run reduced days.
  • Layers, always. It is consistently colder on the water than on shore, in any month.
  • When in doubt, confirm at the source. Schedules vary by season. Every attraction above links to its official site — those pages are current in a way no guide can be.

Times and driving estimates on this page are guidance rather than guarantees — traffic on Route 128 and Route 1A varies enormously by day and season, and we can't promise an arrival time. Hours, fees, reservation rules and seasonal access all change; please check the official website for anything you're making a special trip for.
